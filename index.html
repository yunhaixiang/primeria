Primeria: How to Play

Primeria is a two-player game. Choose a 9 × 9, 11 × 11, or 13 × 13 board in New Game. Both players’ tile piles are visible. The first player to empty their own pile wins.

Moves

Single Drop. Place one tile on any empty square. A five may be placed in isolation. A zero must touch another tile. A zero must be inside every number it belongs to when the placement is complete.

Double Down. Place two tiles, one after another. Each must touch an existing tile, including the one just placed. Adjacency means sharing an edge. A zero may be placed first, but the completed move must enclose it inside its number; it cannot be at either end of any row or column number.

Challenge. Click any tile to select its entire uninterrupted number. Hover to preview the full number; at crossings, move toward a tile’s horizontal or vertical edge, or use the Row and Column buttons. Click an isolated tile once. The number is always read from your own perspective; propose a nontrivial factor. You cannot select only part of a longer number. Single-digit challenges are allowed only for isolated tiles with no edge-adjacent tiles. A correct challenge transfers all but the last digit to your opponent’s pile. The last digit in your reading direction is taxed: it is removed from play and belongs to neither player. For a successful isolated single-digit challenge, that one tile is taxed and none are transferred. You get one factor attempt per three digits, rounded up: 1–3 digits allow 1 attempt, 4–6 allow 2, 7–9 allow 3, 10–12 allow 4, and 13 allow 5. A failed attempt pauses both clocks for three seconds, then your clock resumes for the next attempt of the same number. Quit Challenge ends it as a failure. Success or exhausting all attempts ends the turn, with one increment for the entire challenge. The number 1 cannot be challenged.

Automatic tile removal

After a successful challenge removes a number, any zero left isolated or at the beginning or end of any uninterrupted row or column number is also removed. These zeros go out of play and enter neither player’s pile. Repeat this cleanup if it exposes more zeros. Fives remain on the board even if they become isolated.

Reading direction

On the default board, Player one reads left to right and top to bottom. Player two sits opposite and reads right to left and bottom to top. A challenge uses the challenger’s own reading, and rotating the view does not change either player’s perspective.

The clock runs while choosing tiles and finding factors. Both clocks pause for a three-second challenge result in the move panel, then the next player’s clock resumes automatically. The increment is added after every completed turn. Running out of time or resigning loses the game.

The optional Time Out Rule starts counting completed moves after the chosen duration of active play. The same player must have fewer tiles for the chosen number of consecutive moves. A tie or change of leader resets the streak. Set both values when creating a game; the defaults are 60 minutes (1 hour) and 10 moves.

Your starting pile

Two each of 0 and 5; three each of 2, 4, 6, 8; five each of 1, 3, 7, 9. That’s 36 tiles each.
