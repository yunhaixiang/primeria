THE ART OF PRIMERIA

Small rules.
Prime possibilities.

Two players. A 10 × 10 board. A shared, visible supply of information. The first player to empty their own pile wins.

Three ways to make a move

Single Drop. Place one tile on any empty square. A zero must touch another tile.

Double Down. Place two tiles, one after another. Each must touch an existing tile, including the one just placed. Adjacency means sharing an edge.

Challenge. Select a continuous horizontal or vertical sequence, choose either reading direction, and propose a nontrivial factor. A correct challenge transfers those tiles to your opponent’s pile. An incorrect one uses your turn. The number 1 cannot be challenged.

This version allows contiguous parts of a longer number, interprets leading zeros numerically, and treats 0 as divisible by any factor greater than 1.

The clock runs while choosing tiles and finding factors. Both clocks pause to review a submitted challenge; continue when both players are ready. The increment is added after every completed turn. Running out of time or resigning loses the game.

The optional one-hour rule counts completed turns after one hour of active play: the same player must have fewer tiles for ten consecutive turns.

Your starting pile

Two 0s; three each of 2, 4, 5, 6, 8; five each of 1, 3, 7, 9. That’s 37 tiles each.
