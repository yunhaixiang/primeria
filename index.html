THE ART OF PRIMERIA

Small rules.
Prime possibilities.

Two players. A 12 × 12 board. A shared, visible supply of information. The first player to empty their own pile wins.

Three ways to make a move

Single Drop. Place one tile on any empty square. A zero must touch another tile.

Double Down. Place two tiles, one after another. Each must touch an existing tile, including the one just placed. Adjacency means sharing an edge.

Challenge. Select the entire uninterrupted horizontal or vertical number, choose either reading direction, and propose a nontrivial factor. You cannot select only part of a longer number. Single-digit challenges are allowed only for isolated tiles with no edge-adjacent tiles. A correct challenge transfers those tiles to your opponent’s pile. An incorrect one uses your turn. The number 1 cannot be challenged.

Leading zeros are interpreted numerically. Zero is divisible by any factor greater than 1, but a single zero is challengeable only when it is isolated.

The clock runs while choosing tiles and finding factors. Both clocks pause to review a submitted challenge; continue when both players are ready. The increment is added after every completed turn. Running out of time or resigning loses the game.

The optional Time Out Rule starts counting completed moves after the chosen duration of active play. The same player must have fewer tiles for the chosen number of consecutive moves. A tie or change of leader resets the streak. Set both values when creating a game; the defaults are 60 minutes (1 hour) and 10 moves.

Your starting pile

Two 0s; three each of 2, 4, 5, 6, 8; five each of 1, 3, 7, 9. That’s 37 tiles each.
